Chat Statistics, Explained

A chat export is a small dataset with surprisingly rich structure. This page walks through the statistics that can be computed honestly from a WhatsApp or Telegram export — and what each one can and cannot tell you. ChatWrapped computes all of them locally in your browser.

Compute my chat statistics →

Volume statistics

The foundation: eligible message counts, per participant and overall; word counts (locale-aware tokenization, including stopwords); character counts (counted as grapheme clusters, so emoji and non-Latin scripts count as what they look like); and average message length. Good tools separate system messages and attachment placeholders from these totals — ChatWrapped shows both raw and eligible numbers side by side.

Distribution statistics

Message share per participant (their messages ÷ all eligible messages) answers “who texts more”. Shares are most meaningful in 1:1 and small groups; in a 50-person group the long tail tells its own story — which is why per-participant tables beat pie charts at scale.

Time statistics

  • Monthly and daily trends — the conversation’s seasons and spikes.
  • Hour × weekday heatmap — when the chat is alive; night owls show up immediately.
  • Weekday vs weekend share — work-chat vs weekend-chat energy.
  • Active-day streaks — the longest run of consecutive days with at least one message.
  • Busiest day / month — records with their full dates.

Timezone caveat: exports store local wall-clock time without timezone metadata. ChatWrapped preserves what the sender saw on their clock and computes durations correctly; it never invents a timezone.

Response statistics

Response time needs rules to be honest: ChatWrapped groups rapid consecutive messages by the same person into a “burst”, measures a response from the burst’s first message to the first message from anyone else, and only counts responses within six hours for averages. Median is preferred over mean — one overnight silence shouldn’t dominate. Full rules in the metric guide.

Text statistics

  • Emoji ranking — counted as grapheme clusters so 👍🏽 and 👨‍👩‍👧 are one emoji each; ©/®/™ excluded.
  • Signature words — ranked after removing stopwords, numbers, URLs and email addresses.
  • Question and exclamation rates — small but surprisingly characterful numbers.

What chat statistics cannot tell you

They are counting statistics. They cannot measure affection, loyalty, attention or “toxicity”, and they say nothing about what happens between messages. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling something. ChatWrapped’s stories and awards are deliberately framed as playful activity facts.
