How Chat Analysis Works

Most chat tools show numbers without explaining where they come from. ChatWrapped does the opposite: every metric has a written definition, explicit exclusions and tested edge cases. This page is the short version; the complete reference lives in the repository at docs/analytics/METRIC_DEFINITIONS.md.

Step 1 — Parsing into a normalized model

Both supported formats (WhatsApp TXT, Telegram JSON) are parsed into one internal message model: participant, timestamp, text, type (text / media / system) and attachment flag. This is why the same statistics and stories work identically for both platforms. Parsing handles multiline messages, colons in text, Unicode directionality marks, attachment placeholders, deletion markers and system events.

Step 2 — The eligible pool

Statistics are computed over the eligible pool: non-system messages from included participants, inside the selected date range. You always see both the raw parsed count and the eligible count, so exclusions are never hidden. Group noise (joins, pins, calls, “this message was deleted”) never pollutes the numbers.

Step 3 — Timestamps without timezone lies

Exports store local wall-clock time with no timezone metadata. ChatWrapped interprets the wall clock as UTC — a constant offset — which keeps every duration exact (response times, gaps, streaks) and keeps every bucket faithful to what the sender saw on their clock. No timezone is ever invented. WhatsApp’s ambiguous DD/MM vs MM/DD formats are resolved from unambiguous evidence and flagged for you to confirm when the data can’t decide.

Step 4 — Response times, done honestly

Naive tools treat every message pair as a reply, which collapses when people double-text. ChatWrapped’s rules:

  • Burst: consecutive messages by the same participant within 120 seconds form one burst.
  • Response event: the first message from a different participant after a burst, measured from the burst’s first message.
  • Validity: only responses within 6 hours enter averages and medians; longer silences are tracked separately as the longest gap.

Boundaries are unit-tested (exactly 6 hours counts; 6 hours + 1 ms does not).

Step 5 — Conversation starts

A conversation “starts” after a lull of at least 4 hours since the previous eligible message — plus the very first message of the selection. The starter is credited. This is why “Conversation Starter” awards need at least three starts.

Step 6 — Emoji and words

Emoji are counted as grapheme clusters via Intl.Segmenter: skin tones, ZWJ families, flags and keycaps are one emoji each — never misleading fragments. Words are segmented per locale, lowercased and filtered (stopwords, numbers, URLs, emails) for signature-word rankings.

Step 7 — Stories, records and awards

Slides are generated adaptively from the computed analytics — a slide for a missing metric is skipped, never fabricated. Records require sufficient data; awards have explicit thresholds (published in the metric definitions) and ties are reported as ties. Small samples produce a visible “small sample” note.

What the analysis refuses to do

Your uploaded conversations are processed entirely in your browser. ChatWrapped does not upload your chat files to its servers. And no statistic implies anything about love, loyalty, personality or relationship quality — ChatWrapped counts events; people make meanings.

Curious how this plays out on real data? Open the analyzer — the sample data is synthetic and clearly labeled, so you can explore every screen without exporting anything.
